# Gv Failure Envelope

## Purpose

This document defines the expected operational envelope of the current Gv recoverability framework.

The goal is not to claim universal collapse prediction.

The goal is to explicitly identify:
- where the framework appears theoretically valid,
- where it becomes unreliable,
- and what classes of systems may invalidate its assumptions.

This document exists to constrain claims, reduce overreach, and guide future testing.

---

# Current compressed form

The original multi-component Gv composite has compressed toward a smaller recoverability core centered around:

- autocorrelation slowing (AC)
- recovery lag / slowing return dynamics (RL)

Current interpretation:

A system may become measurably less recoverable before visible functional collapse.

---

# Systems where Gv is expected to work best

The current framework is expected to perform best in systems with:

- gradual approach toward instability
- slowing recovery after perturbation
- quasi-equilibrium dynamics
- observable dominant slow mode
- sufficient sampling continuity
- moderate signal-to-noise ratio

---

# Core assumptions

The current recoverability framework assumes:

1. A meaningful local stability structure exists.
2. Perturbation recovery contains information about resilience.
3. Slowing return dynamics precede collapse.
4. Observable variables partially track the dominant slow mode.
5. Sampling quality is sufficient for rolling estimation.

If these assumptions fail, the framework may fail.

---

# Known failure risks

## Noise-induced transitions
Strong stochastic forcing may push systems across boundaries before measurable slowing appears.

## Rate-induced tipping
Rapid forcing may bypass detectable slowing-return behavior.

## Contagion-driven cascades
Network contagion effects may dominate local recovery dynamics.

## Hidden slow modes
Observed variables may not align with the actual dominant slow mode.

## Strong nonstationarity
External forcing drift may inflate autocorrelation artificially.

## Sparse or irregular sampling
Data gaps and measurement instability may destabilize estimates.

---

# Scientific posture

The framework should remain:
- falsifiable
- compressible
- replaceable if stronger recoverability observables emerge

Dead components should be removed.

Failure cases are valuable results.

---

# Current minimal claim

Systems exhibiting slowing-return dynamics may become measurably less recoverable before visible collapse.

That claim remains under active testing.
