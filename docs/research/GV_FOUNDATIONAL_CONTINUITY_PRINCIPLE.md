# GV Foundational Continuity Principle

## Critical correction

GV should NOT be treated as:
- merely a fitted projection
- an optimized PCA axis
- a post-hoc metric layer

Those may emerge from GV behavior,
but they are not GV itself.

## Foundational position

GV is proposed as a baseline continuity constraint.

Meaning:

A system capable of coherent persistence through time
must preserve sufficient recoverability structure
to remain dynamically continuous.

## Core distinction

Weak interpretation:
GV is a useful recoverability score.

Stronger interpretation:
Recoverability continuity is a primitive structural requirement
for persistent systems.

## Consequence

Observable low-dimensional recoverability geometry
may emerge FROM the continuity constraint,
rather than defining it.

## Operational interpretation

Systems lose GV when:
- recovery collapses
- persistence collapses
- directional degradation dominates
- continuity cannot be restored

## Important scientific restraint

This does NOT prove:
- metaphysics
- theology
- ultimate physical law

It only proposes:

Persistent systems may require continuity-preserving structure.

## Falsification path

The foundational interpretation weakens if:
- persistent systems survive without recoverability continuity
- irreversible degradation is not predictive
- continuity structure fails cross-domain transfer
- persistence does not correlate with recoverability constraints

## Engineering interpretation

GV is not a replacement for domain metrics.

GV is the baseline continuity condition beneath them.
