# GV Foundation Axioms

## Purpose

Keep GV anchored to its foundational intuition while remaining falsifiable.

The goal is not to force a scalar.

The goal is to identify the minimal continuity requirements for persistent existence.

---

# Proposed GV Foundation

## Axiom 1 — Persistence requires continuity

A system cannot persist if all continuity relations collapse.

Persistence may be physical, informational, biological, computational, ecological, or causal.

---

## Axiom 2 — Recoverability is structurally meaningful

Persistent systems must preserve some ability to recover from disturbance.

A system with zero recoverability eventually loses persistence.

---

## Axiom 3 — Irreversible degradation is distinguishable

A continuity-bearing system must distinguish:
- reversible disturbance
from
- irreversible degradation

Otherwise persistence has no operational meaning.

---

## Axiom 4 — Continuity survives harmless transformation

Continuity structure should survive:
- scale transforms
- monotonic transforms
- moderate noise

even if exact coordinates change.

---

## Axiom 5 — Arbitrary coordinates are not sacred

Continuity may depend on causal structure and meaningful axes.

Not all rotations preserve meaning.

---

## Axiom 6 — Local continuity may diverge from global continuity

Healthy local structure does not guarantee global persistence.

Persistent systems may hide global strain behind local stability.

---

## Axiom 7 — Observable behavior may incompletely reveal hidden continuity state

Continuity leakage may appear before full hidden-state reconstruction is possible.

---

# Scientific posture

These axioms are not declared true.

They are proposed as the minimal foundation GV must satisfy
to remain a viable TOE-level continuity hypothesis.

---

# Failure rule

GV weakens if persistent systems systematically violate these axioms.

GV strengthens if these axioms recur across independent domains.
